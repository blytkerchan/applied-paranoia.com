In her book <i>The age of surveillance capitalism</i>, Shoshana Zuboff argues, among other things, that we have a "right to act free of the influence of illegitimate forces that operate outside our awareness to influence, modify, and condition our behavior"[^1]. She goes on to explain how the likes of Google, Facebook (Meta), and Microsoft have us under constant surveillance, build models to predict our behaviorm and submit us to stimuli to modify that behavior in their own interest, and the interests of those who pay for their services.

[^1]: <i>The age of surveillance capitalism</i>, Shoshana Zuboff, part 1, chapter 6, section 6

It is absolutely true that you are undeer increasingly constant surveillance: in all likelihood, you have a smart phone, are seen by a camera at least a few times every day, and are in a room in which the ambient temperature is either directly or indirectly observed. When you go shopping, whether it's on-line or "IRL", your digital overseers know what you've looked at and what you found interesting. Google, Meta, and Microsoft each know more about you than you think and, together, know you better than your Significant Other.

This constant obervation of our behavior, which includes anything related to absolutely any subject that affects our lives, is not because "Mark Zuckerberg wants to know what kind of pasta you prefer": he probably doesn't care about that one bit. This observation instead turns you into a data source. The data you provide, and the metadata associated with it, allows the big advertisers, Google and Meta, to perform extremely targeted advertising, shaping your world into one in which you consume goods and services from their customers. Contrary to what many say -- that when you don't pay for the product, that's because you are the product -- you are not the product. Insights into your behavior and targeted changes to your behavior are the product.

We are in the middle of a vast social experiment that spans the globe. While government surveillance is blatent and omni-present in countries like China, the same surveillance, but more insidious and not run by the government, takes place in the West. Most Western countries have no laws against buying data about their citizens through brokers though, so don't think your government doesn't have you under surveillance as well: they're just not necessarily doing the spying themselves. Most of the time, though, a Western government does't care enough about you individually to look you up in whatever database (or data lake) they store their gathered data in.
